This article  by Mehmet Seremet and Brian Chalkley evaluates students' levels of satisfaction in GIS courses and offers suggestions for where improvements could be made. After collecting data through a questionnaire across multiple institutions in the United Kingdom, they found that for 90% of students, GIS did not play a large role in their decision to study geography. The most notable factor that pushed students to learn GIS was employability, with 34% of students citing that as their reason for employment in the class. Students also found that practical computer exercises were the most valuable part of GIS class, with them enjoying the practical application of geographic theory into practice. 

This article provides important context for the crosswalk: GIS is often learnt as a tool to make oneself more attractive to prospective employers. In this framework, learning workflows across platforms is not only done to enrich one's education, but improve one's employability, adding another potential use case for the crosswalk.


Seremet, M., & Chalkley, B. (2014). Student perspectives on the teaching of Geographical Information Systems (GIS) in geography degrees. Journal of Geography in Higher Education, 39(1), 18-36. DOI: [https://doi.org/10.1080/03098265.2014.963795](https://doi.org/10.1080/03098265.2014.963795)
