In this article by Jeff Howarth in Cartographic Perspectives, Howarth uses the example of revising the class materials of a late colleague to explain the importance of Cognitive Load Theory in GIS. In his redesigned labs, he emphasized the importance of using worked examples to introduce GIS tools and concepts prior to students tackling them in the lab. This, coupled with the discussion of analogous practice problems in the preceding lectures helps students understand the conceptual reasoning behind a particular tool prior to them using it - avoiding 'buttonology' (only teaching students what buttons to press)

Howarth's article provides substantial framing for what the crosswalk is attempting to do. Providing learners with a worked example of a workflow on a platform they know aids the learner in understanding how to accomplish the same workflow in the second platform, assuming they understand the conceptual steps being taken. Thus, it will be important to utilize Minimal GIS concepts and thoroughly explain what a workflow is doing to help the learning process. 

Howarth, J. (2015). Learning by solving problems:   Cognitive load theory and the re-design of an introductory GIS course. Cartographic Perspectives, (80), 18-34. https://doi.org/10.14714/cp80.1320
